Connect a Bluetooth or WiFi ELM327 to your laptop

An ELM327 is the cheapest and most common OBD2 adapter: it works with any car with OBD2/EOBD (in Europe, petrol cars from 2001 and diesels from 2003-2004). They come as USB, Bluetooth and WiFi. This guide explains how to connect them to a laptop.

How it works: a serial port without the cable

A Bluetooth ELM327 behaves like a serial cable without the cable. When you pair it, the system creates a virtual serial port: a COM port on Windows or /dev/rfcomm0 on Linux. The program talks to that port just as it would to a USB adapter. That is why in a diagnostic program you do not choose “Bluetooth” but that port.

A WiFi ELM327 creates its own network; the laptop joins it and the program talks to the adapter at an IP address and port, nearly always 192.168.0.10:35000.

Which type to buy

  • USB: the most reliable and fastest. It shows up straight away as a COM port or /dev/ttyUSB0.
  • Classic Bluetooth (SPP): convenient and works well on Windows and Linux.
  • WiFi: works, but while you are on its network the laptop has no internet.
  • BLE-only adapters (Bluetooth Low Energy, some sold for iPhone) do not create a serial port on a computer.
  • Many clones claim “v2.1” and are old, incomplete versions. They usually work, but some fail with certain cars.

Windows 10 and 11

  1. Plug the ELM327 into the OBD connector and switch the ignition on: its light comes on.
  2. Go to Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device → Bluetooth and choose the adapter. It is usually called OBDII, OBD2 or V-LINK. If it asks for a PIN, try 1234, 0000 or 6789.
  3. Check which port it got: More Bluetooth settings → COM Ports tab, the Outgoing one (for example, COM5). It also appears in Device Manager → Ports (COM & LPT) as “Standard Serial over Bluetooth link”.
  4. In the program, choose that port and click Connect.

It is normal for Windows to show it as “Paired” rather than “Connected”: the connection opens when the program opens the port. Windows usually creates two COM ports; if one does not answer, try the other.

Linux

  1. Plug the ELM327 in and switch the ignition on.
  2. Pair it from your desktop's Bluetooth settings or in a terminal with bluetoothctl: scan on, and when it appears, pair AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF and trust AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF (the MAC is the one shown while scanning).
  3. Linux does not create the serial port by itself. Create it with sudo rfcomm bind 0 AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF and you get /dev/rfcomm0. Repeat it after a reboot.
  4. Your user needs permission to use serial ports: sudo usermod -aG dialout $USER (on Arch, uucp) and log in again.
  5. In the program, choose /dev/rfcomm0 and click Connect.

If you do not have the rfcomm command: on Fedora it is in the bluez-deprecated package; on Arch, in bluez-deprecated-tools. On Debian, Ubuntu and Mint it comes with bluez.

WiFi ELM327

  1. Plug the adapter in and switch the ignition on. It creates a WiFi network called, for example, “WiFi_OBDII”, “OBDII” or “V-LINK”.
  2. Connect the laptop to that network.
  3. In OBD2Desk, choose the “WiFi · 192.168.0.10:35000” port. If your adapter uses another address (for example, 192.168.1.10), type it as ip:port.

While you are on the adapter's network you have no internet, so the AI assistant will not work at the same time.

If it does not answer

  • It only accepts one connection at a time. If you have used it with your phone (Torque, Car Scanner…), close that app or turn the phone's Bluetooth off.
  • “The vehicle does not respond to OBD2”: switch the ignition on. If it is a VAG car from before 2004, it probably has no generic OBD2 and you need a KKL cable.
  • Pre-CAN cars (up to ~2005-2007): they speak OBD2 over the K-line and the ELM327's automatic search sometimes fails. OBD2Desk then tries KWP2000 and ISO 9141 one by one; it takes up to a minute.
  • If nothing works, the adapter may be a clone that does not handle the K-line well. A good-quality USB one rules that out.
