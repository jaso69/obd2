OBD2 fault codes: full list and meaning

OBD2 fault codes are the ones any car with OBD2/EOBD (in Europe, petrol cars from 2001 and diesels from 2003-2004) gives to a reader such as an ELM327. Here is the meaning of the 9533 generic codes, the ones that mean the same for every make.

How to read an OBD2 code

  • The letter gives the system: P powertrain (engine and transmission), B body (airbags, seats, lighting), C chassis (brakes, ABS, suspension) and U the network between control units.
  • The first digit says who defines it: 0 (and also P2, P34 and U3) are generic codes from the SAE J2012 standard, the same for every make; 1 and 2 (except P2) are manufacturer-specific.
  • The rest gives the subsystem and the fault. In P0 codes the second digit groups them: 1 and 2 fuel and air, 3 ignition, 4 emissions, 5 speed and idle, 6 computer, 7 to 9 transmission, and A to E hybrid and electric.

Manufacturer codes (P1xxx, B1xxx…) differ from one make to another and are not in this list. For Volkswagen, Audi, SEAT and Skoda see the list of VAG fault codes.

Type the code (P0420) or a word from the description.

Before replacing parts

  • Write the faults down before clearing them, with the fault type and, on OBD2, the freeze frame data (engine speed, temperature and load when the fault happened).
  • Clear them and see whether they come back. A fault that does not return after several starts may be old; one that comes back straight away is current.
  • Check the wiring and connectors first when the fault is a short, an open circuit or intermittent.
  • Look at the component's measured values with the engine running: a sender giving an absurd value (−40 °C coolant, for example) confirms the fault.
